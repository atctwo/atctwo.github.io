In July, I went to a hacker camp in a field in England called Electromagnetic Field.  Despite knowing about the camp for a while now this was my first year, and my first festival (hacker or otherwise)!  Given how surreal of an experience it was I ended up taking a bunch of notes in an attempt to process it, and I thought it might be nice to tidy them up a bit to share my thoughts.

This post will be a fairly direct translation of my internal notes, with some tidying to make it more readable.  This means it probably won't flow very well, but I still think it would be nice to share my thoughts and experiences.

I don't really know where to begin describing what EMF actually _is_, so if you've never heard of it I would direct you to [their website](https://www.emfcamp.org/).  Also, a bunch of pictures I took at the festival are available on [this here webby site](/photos/electromagnetic-field-2026) if you want a more visual introduction (and some of these pictures are used here)!

# What to do at EMF
*deciding what to do over the four days*

## See stuff!
I was warned about this going into it the event, but there's so much stuff to do that you can't do all of it.  I think it surprised me just how much this was the case - I didn't end up going to half the talks i wanted to just because I ended up doing other stuff like talking to people in other villages.

This was especially true on the Thursday (the first full day), where the [heatwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_Kingdom_heatwaves#July_heatwave) really got to me and I struggled to stick to any real plan.  As the event went on I got better at handling the heat, and it did cool down a bit, but I still ended up feeling a bit dazed.

I'm glad the activities that I didn't end up doing were mostly talks, since those were (mostly) recorded and are available at [media.ccc.de](https://media.ccc.de/c/emf2026).  This also meant that the things I did get to do were things you could *only* do at the event.  All the social events, meetups, workshops, markets, NullSector raves, sticker hunting - these are things you just can't replicate online.  Most of all was the *wandering* - I spent hours just walking around, by myself or with friends, just seeing what people were doing, wandering into villages and striking up conversations with strangers, dialling random numbers on public phones, finding new art installations, taking [World'o'Techno](https://jarkman.co.uk/catalog/robots/worldotechno.htm) for a walk, trying [pilk](https://pilk.uk/) - these are deeply unique experiences you could only really have at an event like EMF, and that I will never forget.

## Make stuff!
When my friends and I were initially planning our trip to EMF, I had the idea of replicating They Might Be Giants' [Dial-a-Song](https://tmbw.net/wiki/Dial-A-Song) service (dial a number to get a new TMBG song every day!) on the on-site [phone system](https://phones.emfcamp.org/).  I had actually started research on how to build such a system, but weeks before the event I discovered that someone else already had the idea, and had already implemented theirs.

I felt pretty bummed out because that was The Thing that I thought I could contribute to EMF.  I didn't like the idea that I was "just" going to be a an attendee without bringing anything to enrich the event.

EMF is an event that is made of what people bring to it, but that doesn't mean that *everyone* has to bring something.  It's perfectly fine to attend just to explore the festival and hang out with friends!  I would argue that participation in the event is a form of contribution in and of itself, and in the end I had an amazing time just being part of EMF.

I did come up with a few ideas for other EMF-specific projects I could bring, but I had pretty much no time to work on them.  They'll have to wait for next time...

# Logistics
*thoughts on how we organised this year, equipment I should have (and shouldn't have) brought*

## Transport
One of the things stopping me from going to EMF in previous years was transport - there are trains to the nearby village of Ledbury and a shuttle bus to the site, but the full EMF experience demands more equipment than can be packed into a hand luggage bag (not to mention the camping equipment you need to bring).

This year however, I was lucky enough to be attending with a few local friends, so we were able to pack all our stuff into one friend's tiny car.  We did manage to get it all squeezed in but with virtually no space remaining.  For next time, we will almost definitely need a bigger vehicle.

## What to bring
Having never gone before, I struggled to know what to bring.  There's a lot of equipment that I knew that I *would* need, but also a lot that I thought I *might* need, and balancing what to bring based on how likely I thought it would be that I would use it was difficult!  Knowing what I did end up using, I think I'll have a better idea for next time what to bring.

As a few examples:

- I did end up bringing my RJ45/RJ11 crimp tool plus connectors.  I'm glad I did because I *almost* used them, but looking back I also would have been fine if I didn't bring them.  
- I brought a spare Mikrotik router + a 5 port switch.  I didn't end up using them, and honestly I didn't think I was going to, but having them gave me some comfort.  For next time I think it might be better to bring a travel router
- I was going to bring a Cisco VoIP phone and an ATA, but I dropped them because I thought I might be too busy to mess around with them (i was right)
- I brought a HackRF, which I'm sad that I didn't end up using.  I did want to do some radio hacking stuff but I ended up being too busy with everything else.  Despite this, it's such a versatile tool that I would bring it again
- I brought like 6-7 USB-C cables and 5 CAT5e cables.  I ended up using *most* of them, and they were pretty lightweight
- I brought a 15m extension cord with a 16A connector + an inline RCD, for power to my tent.  My tent ended up right beside a power distro crate so 15m was enough, but only just about.  This ended up being one of the largest things I brought, and unfortunately there isn't really a way to scale it down

EMF is a camping festival, so it's important to bring the right camping equipment:

- I brought my green flask which I kept topped up with Coke Zero.  My advice is to not drink exclusively cola, but the important thing is to *bring a flask and keep it topped up*.  The event had multiple free water taps, and they were very necessary in the heat.  I ended up refilling my 800 mL flask at least three times a day.
- my tent ended up being very very warm.  I suppose this is ideal for a tent (you normally want it to keep heat when outdoors), but it got to the point where it was cooler outside than it was inside.  for most of the day I actually couldn't stay in my tent for too long because it was just too warm.
- for this reason it's recommended that you cover your tent in foil blankets / space blankets, to reflect the heat.  I did try this but I think I made a few mistakes
  - the blankets I used were silver on both sides, meaning heat was reflected away from the tent but also trapped inside.  you can get blankets which are silver on one side and gold on the other which allow heat to permeate in one direction - I think these might have worked better
  - I put one blanket on the south face of my tent; I think might have been better to put the blankets on the east and west sides since that's where the heat will mostly be coming from.

Aside from equipment, it's important to be prepared for any combination of English weather:

- due to the heat, I'm **very** glad I brought a tank top and shorts.  the weather forecast was kinda all over the place the week leading up to the event so light clothes were a last minute addition, and I *really* wish I had have brought more
- additionally, I very quickly became best friends with suncream!  i was actually worried I was going to run out
- the next thing you learn is how cold it can get at night.  having a decent sleeping bag is pretty important, but if you plan to stay up until 4am yapping each night like I did, bring a hoodie or something warm!  (even if it's like 35° during the day)
- a good rule is to prepare for every type of weather!

# Community
*thoughts on my first real-life encounter with the EMF community*

## A culture of sharing

A pattern in open-source circles is that people dedicate a lot of time (and money) without expecting much in return.  People do stuff they like because it's fun!  EMF seems to attract these types of people, and the whole event feels like it's had countless quantities of time and effort and love poured into it for the love of bringing it into existence!

For example, the various art installations around site don't have admission fees - the people who make them put hours of work and probably a lot of money into creating these things, then leave them to be experienced freely.

While this culture of freely sharing for the purposes of fun can be found online, it's rare to see it in such force in real life.  To see and talk to the people who partake in and encourage these ways of living is endearing!

## Queer community
I don't really have access to hard data to back this up but entirely based on how I read the expressions of everyone else there, EMF was a very queer event.  (Does the fact that the place was covered in pride flags count as hard data?)  The event isn't *primarily* a queer event, but the overlap between the queer and tech communities and the spirit of freedom of expression that EMF nurtures makes it a place where queer people can feel safe and open.

I've spent a lot of time in local queer spaces in and around Belfast; these spaces have their own very strong communities and were deeply important for me when I was exploring my identity.  The nature of spaces like this is that they are sort of spatially finite - excluding online spaces, we have regular meetups at a regular location, but outside of that it's the regular world.

EMF is a unique example of what it's like to have a queer *population*.  Where in conventional spaces you might spend a few hours around people in your community, EMF takes place in a large field for the guts of a week.  I was living in a space where much of the population, most of the strangers around me were the same as me!  I've never experienced that before!!

I think this affords people a fundamental level of comfort, knowing that the entire site across the entire duration of the event is a permanent safe space.  Certainly in my case I felt totally at ease dressing in a tank top and shorts, which is something I've always been deeply anxious of doing in the "real world".

The weeks after EMF were actually pretty jarring, having to return to living in a world that doesn't have that base level of people like me.  Even in my regular spaces it feels like something is missing - my local queer spaces don't really have much of an overlap between the tech community, and my local tech spaces don't really have much overlap with the queer community.  At least in Belfast, there seems to be a lack in spaces that bring together *both*.

EMF has given me a taste of what such a community can look like, and I feel inspired!  If you can't find your community, *why not make one*?

# Until next time
From what I remember, there were about two times during the event where I wasn't really sure if I would want to do EMF in 2028.  Both times I was pretty badly affected by the heat and I was kinda anxious that I couldn't hide in my tent.

On the other hand, there were countless times that I felt certain I wanted to return.  There are times which feel uncomfortable, it requires *a lot* of preparation, and its **very** expensive, but with everything else in mind I would agree to do it all again in a heartbeat.  All the negative experiences became virtually negligible when I consider all the unique, surreal, and inspiring experiences I had.

EMF is a demonstration of what maker culture can look like in it's most unrestrained form, the communities it can build, the freedom it can afford, the love that can be shared, and the unhinged affronts to engineering that we all have the power to make happen.

I'll see you at Electromagnetic Field 2028!
